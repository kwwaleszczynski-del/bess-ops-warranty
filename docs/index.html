Grid battery operations against warranty and LTSA

An asset manager's view of a battery: what the meter says about round-trip efficiency once the auxiliaries are counted, how available it was by the contract's formula, how hard it is cycled against the warranty conditions, and what the capacity tests say about when it will need augmentation. The same estimators run on every grid battery in Australia's National Electricity Market (public AEMO data), are checked against an operator's own published data, and are verified on a synthetic battery with known truth. Code and method: github.com/kwwaleszczynski-del/bess-ops-warranty.

Warranty, LTSA and liquidated-damages terms in the examples are illustrative placeholders, not a reading of any contract. Market data show what an operator offers and what the connection point measures; they do not show auxiliaries behind the meter, temperatures or capacity tests (see Limitations in the README).

1. Round-trip efficiency of every NEM battery

RTE at AEMO's SCADA point over local days with at least 0.3 cycles, corrected for the change of the stored energy each battery reports to AEMO (BMS recalibration jumps removed, months with an inconsistent report left out). Bars span the 25th to 75th percentile of the days, the dot is the median, the tick the whole year. Crosses: the conversion efficiency from a regression of the same reported energy in the companion project bess-soc-soh-degradation, which separates standby losses; circles: the efficiency factors registered with AEMO.

2. Availability: what is offered and what is physically there

Capacity-weighted availability from the discharge availability each battery offers (DISPATCHLOAD.AVAILABILITY against registered MW), over all intervals and over intervals in which it held more than 10 % of its registered energy, and the technical availability: the physical availability each participant declares with its bids (BIDPEROFFER_D.PASAAVAILABILITY), the lower of the discharge and charge sides against registered MW, as an LTSA's capacity formula would count it (near the SoC limits only the direction that can still operate counts). Batteries in service for at least six months, from the first month they declared 80 % of their power (commissioning before). Offers mix outages with energy limits, capacity held back under other contracts (system-integrity schemes) and bidding style; the declaration does not, but it is still the participant's statement, not a measurement.

3. One battery over the year

Upper envelope: the 99.5th percentile of the reported stored energy per month, as a share of the registered energy (it follows the operator's SoC window, not only the cells). Monthly RTE is shown only for months with a consistent stored-energy report.

4. Fleet table

Click a column to sort. Operational months: at least one cycle and 20 days of data.

5. Validation with the operator's own data: New England BESS

ACEN publishes 5-minute power and SoC of both 100 MW sections. Computing the daily RTE three ways separates the two things that differ between an operator's data and market data: the metering point (A vs B) and the source of the stored energy (B vs C).

6. Verification on a synthetic battery with known truth

Two years of 15-minute EMS data of a 50 MW / 100 MWh battery simulated from a physical energy balance (PCS 0.975 and battery 0.98 one way, auxiliaries driven by ambient temperature, outages, capacity fade, four capacity tests at different cell temperatures, injected warranty violations), exported with naive Europe/Warsaw timestamps including both DST changes.

7. Example monthly report

The report the tool writes for one month of the synthetic battery: KPIs against the guarantees, warranty status and violations, illustrative liquidated damages per LTSA year, capacity tests, SoH forecast and the energy balance. The same report as an Excel workbook (openpyxl, conditional formatting): download .xlsx.
